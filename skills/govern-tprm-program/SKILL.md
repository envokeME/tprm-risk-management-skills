# Govern TPRM Program

## Objective

Create a governable operating model that connects the complete third-party
population to lifecycle decisions, accountable authority, evidence, oversight,
and improvement.

## Required inputs

- Business objectives, risk appetite, obligations, and existing policies
- Third-party population, services, owners, tiers, lifecycle state, and systems
- Current RACI, workflows, exceptions, incidents, findings, metrics, and audits
- Procurement, legal, privacy, security, resilience, finance, and business views

## Workflow

1. Define program scope, objectives, terminology, decision rights, and regulated
   overlays. Distinguish all third parties from the risk-prioritized population.
2. Baseline the current state with `references/program-governance-rules.md`.
3. Define one authoritative inventory with legal entity, service/use case,
   owner, tier, criticality, data/access, dependencies, lifecycle state,
   decisions, findings, evidence dates, contract dates, and exit needs.
4. Design lifecycle gates from planning through termination, including change
   triggers and emergency paths.
5. Establish RACI and delegated authorities for tiering, exceptions, risk
   acceptance, contract fallback, incidents, renewals, and termination.
6. Define risk-based service levels, quality controls, independent review,
   issue management, concentration/fourth-party oversight, and training.
7. Build outcome and health metrics; avoid treating questionnaire volume as risk
   reduction.
8. Copy `assets/tprm-operating-model.md`; prioritize improvements by risk,
   dependency, effort, owner, date, and verification.

## NIST alignment

Use `references/nist-alignment.md` for NIST CSF 2.0 `GV.SC-01`, `GV.SC-02`,
`GV.SC-03`, `GV.SC-04`, `GV.SC-09`, `GV.RR-02`, and `GV.OV-03`. Use NIST SP
800-161 Rev. 1 for multilevel strategy, policy, plans, and risk integration.

## Boundaries

- Do not claim program effectiveness from document design alone.
- Do not fabricate inventory completeness, metrics, evidence, obligations, or
  approval.
- Do not present sample RACI, tiers, or metrics as mandatory.
- Identify independence conflicts and missing authority explicitly.
- Keep policy approval, risk appetite, funding, and acceptance with an authorized human governance body.

## Quality check

Confirm every lifecycle gate has entry/exit criteria, owner, authority, system
record, escalation, evidence, quality review, and measurable outcome.
